GSM — grams per square metre — is the first number on almost every towel quotation, and the one first-time buyers lean on hardest when comparing suppliers. It is a real measurement, not a marketing figure, but it answers a narrower question than most buyers think it does.

## What GSM actually measures

GSM is the weight of the fabric per square metre, nothing more. It correlates with absorbency and hand-feel, but it is not the same thing as either — two towels at the same GSM can feel and perform differently depending on the yarn and the weave, which is why comparing suppliers on GSM alone misses most of what actually matters.

## The ranges buyers typically encounter

**300–400 GSM** sits at the lighter end for terry towelling. It dries fastest, costs less to launder and ship, and is common where laundry turnaround is tight — high-occupancy budget and mid-scale properties running towels through the wash daily. It absorbs less per towel, which some guests notice and most do not.

**500–600 GSM** is the range most full-service hotels specify. It absorbs comfortably for a full-body dry, holds up to repeated commercial laundering without the pile matting down too fast, and dries in a reasonable cycle on hotel-grade equipment. This is the middle ground buyers default to when they haven't yet decided which way to lean.

**600–900 GSM** is the luxury and spa end. It feels substantial the moment a guest picks it up, absorbs the most per towel, and is what five-star and boutique properties use deliberately as a felt marker of quality. The tradeoff is real: it takes longer through the dryer, costs more per wash cycle, and weighs more to ship — for a property running high turnover, that tradeoff adds up fast.

## Why heavier is not automatically better

A heavier towel is not a better towel by default — it is a different set of tradeoffs, and the right choice depends on how the property actually runs. A resort with a slow, high-touch turnover and guests staying multiple nights benefits from the heavier end. A business hotel or serviced apartment operator running tight same-day turnaround, with laundry cost and dryer time as real line items, is often better served lighter than they first assume — and most guests do not consciously register the difference the way the buyer expects them to.

## Yarn changes what GSM feels like

This is the part a spec sheet doesn't show: two towels at the same GSM can feel noticeably different depending on the yarn. Zero-twist yarn gives a softer hand and higher absorbency for its weight, because the fibres are looser and more surface area touches the skin. Combed ringspun cotton, twisted tighter, gives a firmer hand but holds its pile through more commercial wash cycles before it starts to mat down. A 500 GSM zero-twist towel and a 500 GSM ringspun towel are not the same product wearing the same number — one is optimised for guest-facing softness, the other for laundry-room durability.

## What actually decides the number

The honest answer is that GSM should follow from three things a buyer already knows about their own operation: how often the towel gets laundered, how long guests typically use it before it's replaced in the room, and what the property is trying to signal — value, consistency, or indulgence. A specification chosen backwards from those three answers tends to hold up; one picked because a number "sounds premium" tends to get revisited after the first laundry cycle.
