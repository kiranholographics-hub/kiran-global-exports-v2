A buyer sizing their first order usually starts from a single number — "how many towels fit in a container?" — and the honest answer is that it depends on enough variables that the number is worth confirming for your specific order rather than assumed from a general rule.

## The containers themselves

A standard 20ft container holds roughly 33 cubic metres internally; a standard 40ft holds roughly 67 cubic metres, and a 40ft high-cube (taller by about 30cm) holds roughly 76. A 40ft isn't simply double a 20ft in usable capacity once pallet or floor-loading patterns are accounted for, which is the first place the "just double it" assumption breaks down.

## What actually decides the count

Volume, not weight, is almost always the limiting factor for towels — cotton terry is bulky relative to its weight, so a container reaches its cubic capacity well before it reaches its legal weight limit. That makes carton size and packing method, not GSM alone, the biggest levers on how many pieces actually fit.

**Carton dimensions.** Two cartons holding the same number of towels can occupy noticeably different volume depending on how tightly the towels are folded and compressed inside. A carton spec agreed early — dimensions, pieces per carton, gross weight — is what a freight forwarder actually uses to calculate loadability, not the product specification alone.

**Folding and compression.** Towels shipped flat-folded take up more space than towels vacuum-compressed or roll-packed, where that's suitable for the product. This is a real trade-off: compression improves container utilisation but adds a processing step and can affect how a towel looks on unpacking, which is why it's usually a private-label or bulk-hospitality decision rather than a retail-ready one where presentation in the box matters.

**GSM and construction.** A heavier towel takes up more volume per piece than a lighter one at the same dimensions, so a 600 GSM range fits fewer pieces per container than a 400 GSM range at the same size — one more reason the GSM decision (see our guide on choosing between 400/500/600 GSM) has a knock-on effect on landed cost per piece, not just on laundry and durability.

## Why this matters before you commit to a quantity

A buyer who orders "a 40ft container" without confirming carton spec and packing method against their actual product can end up with a shipment that doesn't fill the container efficiently — paying for space that ships empty — or, less often, a product that doesn't fit the way they assumed and needs a second container. Either way, it's a conversation worth having against your actual specification, not a number to assume from a general container-capacity chart.
