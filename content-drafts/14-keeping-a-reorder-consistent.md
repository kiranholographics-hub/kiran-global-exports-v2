A first towel order gets a lot of attention: a specification agreed line by line, a sample approved, a lab dip signed off. The reorder six months later usually gets an email saying "same as last time". That is where most consistency problems start — not because anyone was careless, but because "same as last time" only works if both sides are holding the same reference.

## Why a reorder drifts more easily than a first order

A first order is checked against something physical you approved weeks earlier. A reorder is checked against whatever survived from the first one — sometimes a spec sheet, sometimes an invoice line, sometimes memory. Meanwhile the inputs have moved on: a new cotton crop, a new yarn lot, a new dye lot, a different week on the finishing line. Each of those is normal, and each can move hand-feel, weight or shade slightly. None of it is a problem if the reorder is checked against the original approved reference. All of it adds up if it is checked against a description.

## What to keep on your side after the first order

- The approved lab dip swatch for every colour — the physical one, not a photo of it
- One approved sample piece from the first order, stored away from light
- The final agreed specification: GSM, size, construction, border, finish and packing
- Any packaging, labelling or carton-marking approvals, if those were part of the order

The lab dip swatch matters most. A colour description is not a colour, and a swatch that has been sitting on a sunny desk is not the colour you approved either.

## What to send with the reorder

Send the reference, not just the order quantity: the specification as finally agreed, the colour reference by its approved lab dip, and — if anything about the first shipment was not quite right — say so now. A reorder is the cheapest point to correct something, because nothing has been woven or dyed yet.

If you are changing anything, even something small like a carton count or a label position, treat it as a specification change and confirm it in writing. Small changes slipped into a "repeat" order are the ones nobody checks for.

## What to ask any supplier before a second order

- Is it the same mill as the first order? A different mill is a different product, whatever the spec sheet says.
- What will the bulk be checked against — the first order's approved sample and lab dip, or a new reference?
- Will a fresh lab dip be dyed against the original approved swatch before bulk dyeing?
- Will you see a pre-shipment sample from the new bulk run before it ships?

A supplier who answers these directly is managing consistency. One who says "it will be the same" without saying how is relying on luck.

## What still varies, even when everything is done right

Natural fibre and dyed textiles carry normal batch-to-batch tolerance. Two correctly made batches can sit a fraction apart in shade or hand-feel. What matters is that the difference stays within a reasonable tolerance of the approved reference — and that is something you can only judge by comparing the new pre-shipment sample with the piece you kept, side by side, under the same light.
