"Terry or woven?" is a slightly misleading question, because terry is woven as well. What separates the two is the loop: in terry, an extra set of yarns is woven in to stand up as loops on the surface of the towel; in a flat weave — waffle, honeycomb and similar constructions — there are no loops, and the texture comes from the weave pattern itself. Almost everything a buyer notices about the towel follows from that one difference.

## What the loop does

Loops multiply the surface area of cotton in contact with water and skin. That is where terry's absorbency comes from, and most of its softness: a loop pile soaks up quickly and feels full in the hand. The same loops are also why terry is heavier, bulkier when folded, and slower to dry — more cotton surface holds more water, and a dense pile releases it slowly.

Loops can also snag. A ring, a zip or a rough edge can pull a loop out of the surface, which shows as a stray thread on an otherwise good towel. In a well-made terry towel with a tight ground weave this is uncommon and easy to trim; in a loosely woven one it happens often.

## What a flat weave does instead

A waffle or honeycomb weave builds texture into the cloth without loops. The pockets in the weave still hold some water, and the open structure lets air through, so the towel dries much faster and folds much thinner. It feels crisper rather than plush, and it does not snag the way loops do.

The trade is absorbency. Per square metre, a flat weave holds less water than terry, so it suits uses where a quick wipe, fast drying or low bulk matters more than a deep, soaking dry.

## The middle option: one-side terry

Some towels keep the loop on one face and finish the other face in a smooth, flat weave. That makes the towel noticeably thinner in a stack than double-sided terry of the same weight, at the cost of roughly half the absorbent surface. It works well where the look of a slim, neatly folded towel matters — a minimal bathroom, a display or amenity role — rather than as the main bath towel.

## Why GSM does not compare across constructions

GSM — grams per square metre — is a fair way to compare two terry towels, or two waffle towels. It is a poor way to compare one with the other. A terry towel carries much of its weight in the loops; a flat weave carries it in the ground cloth. Two towels at the same GSM can feel, absorb and dry very differently, so compare like with like, and judge across constructions by handling a sample rather than reading a number.

## Which one for which use

- High-volume hotel bath programmes: double-sided terry, usually ringspun, for absorbency that survives commercial laundering.
- Spas, gyms, pools and travel ranges: waffle or other flat weaves, where dry time and pack size matter more than maximum absorbency.
- Minimal, low-bulk bathroom presentation or amenity towels: one-side terry.
- Bathrobes: terry for a warm, absorbent post-bath robe; waffle for a lighter robe worn between spa treatments.
