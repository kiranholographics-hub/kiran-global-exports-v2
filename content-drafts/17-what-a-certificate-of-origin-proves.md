A certificate of origin turns up in almost every export shipment's paperwork, alongside the commercial invoice and packing list, and it is easy to treat it as a general stamp of legitimacy. It is narrower than that. It answers one question for customs — which country the goods count as coming from — and that answer matters more in some shipments than others.

## What it is

A certificate of origin is a document stating the country in which the goods were produced, issued or endorsed by an authorised body rather than simply declared by the seller. Your customs authority uses it to decide which duty rate and which trade rules apply to the shipment.

There are two broad kinds. A non-preferential certificate simply states origin — useful wherever an importing country wants evidence of where goods were made. A preferential certificate is issued under a specific trade agreement between the exporting and importing countries, in the format that agreement requires, and is what lets an importer claim a reduced or zero duty rate where the agreement provides one.

## What it proves

That the goods meet the origin rules of the importing country — which, for textiles, usually turn on where the product was substantially made rather than where every input came from. For a towel woven and finished in India, the certificate establishes India as the country of origin for customs purposes, and with a preferential certificate, that the goods qualify under the relevant agreement.

## What it does not prove

- **Quality.** It says nothing about weight, hand-feel, colour or construction. That is what an approved sample and a pre-shipment sample are for.
- **Product certifications.** OEKO-TEX, GOTS or any other standard is certified separately, by a different body, with its own certificate. A certificate of origin is not evidence of either.
- **Where the raw cotton was grown.** Origin rules generally follow where the product was made, not the farm the fibre came from. If fibre origin matters to your programme, that is a separate question to ask.
- **Social or environmental compliance.** Audits such as amfori BSCI cover working conditions; the certificate of origin does not.

## Why the right form matters before shipping

Certificates are issued per shipment, and a preferential certificate only works in the exact format the trade agreement specifies. If your country has an agreement with India that covers towels, ask your customs broker whether a preferential certificate would reduce your duty and which form it needs — and tell your supplier before the goods ship. Correcting or reissuing a certificate after departure is sometimes possible but rarely quick, and a shipment that arrives without the right one may be cleared at the full rate.

If no agreement applies, a standard certificate is usually all that is needed, and many buyers' brokers ask for it as routine.

## What to tell your supplier

- Whether your broker needs a standard or a preferential certificate
- If preferential, which agreement and which form
- The consignee and importer details exactly as they should appear
