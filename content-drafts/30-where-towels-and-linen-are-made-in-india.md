"Made in India" covers very different factories. A terry towel, a cotton tablecloth and a bath mat are often made in different states, by mills that specialise in one thing. Knowing which centre makes what helps a buyer ask the right question of any Indian supplier: not only "can you make this?" but "where is it made, and by whom?" This guide describes the main towel and home-textile centres, what each is known for, and what that means for sourcing.

It is a general overview for buyers, not a ranking of places or companies. Every centre has good and poor manufacturers, and the checks at the end apply wherever a supplier is.

## Solapur, Maharashtra: terry towels

Solapur, in southern Maharashtra, has woven cotton for well over a century and is one of India's best-known centres for terry towels. "Solapur terry towels" carry a Geographical Indication, which recognises the product as tied to the place. The city also gave its name to the Solapur chaddar, a woven cotton bedsheet.

Solapur's mills range from small powerloom units to established terry weavers, making bath towels, hand towels, bath sheets, bathrobes, kitchen and beach towels for the domestic market and for export. For a buyer, Solapur is the natural place to ask about a terry programme: that is what its weavers, dyers and finishers are set up to make.

Our own towels and bathrobes are woven in Solapur, at our partner mill V P Mundada, which has been weaving there since 1972.

## Karur, Tamil Nadu: bed, table and kitchen linen

Karur is one of India's main export centres for woven home textiles: bed linen, table linen, kitchen linen, curtains and furnishing fabrics. Much of its output is flat-woven rather than terry, and many of its exporters work directly with retailers and importers abroad on their own designs.

If your order is a tablecloth, a napkin set, an apron or a bedsheet rather than a towel, a Karur manufacturer is often the right specialist. Our organic cotton linen is made by our partner Cotonex in Karur, certified to GOTS.

## Panipat, Haryana: rugs, blankets, mats and recycled yarn

Panipat, north of Delhi, is known for blankets, rugs, bath mats, throws, cushions and furnishings, and for recycling textile waste into yarn. If a range includes bath mats, rugs or recycled-yarn products, Panipat is one of the first places Indian buyers look.

## Erode, Tirupur and Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu: weaving, knitting and spinning

Western Tamil Nadu is one of the densest textile regions in India. Erode is a major cloth market and weaving centre, Tirupur is the country's best-known knitwear centre, and Coimbatore has a long history of spinning mills. For towel buyers, the region matters mostly as a source of yarn and of woven fabric. A supplier there may weave towels too, but ask, because many make other textiles.

## The large integrated mills

Some of India's largest towel producers are integrated companies, with spinning, weaving, dyeing and finishing at one site, in states such as Gujarat, Punjab and Madhya Pradesh. They are built for very large, repeat volumes for big retailers. A smaller importer, or a buyer who needs a custom design and a short first run, often finds a specialist mill in a centre like Solapur a better fit.

## Why the centre matters to a buyer

- **Specialisation:** a mill that weaves terry every day will usually make a better towel than one that does it occasionally. Ask what share of the factory's output is the product you are buying.
- **Matched programmes:** if towels, robes and bath mats come from different centres, the white may not match and reorders drift. Ask where each item in your range will be made.
- **Who makes it:** many Indian exporters source from one or more mills rather than weaving everything under their own roof. That is not a problem in itself; what matters is that the supplier tells you which mill makes your goods, in which city. We name ours at the end of this guide.
- **Certificates:** a certificate belongs to a named site. A GOTS or OEKO-TEX certificate held by a mill in one city does not cover goods made in another. Check that the certificate names the factory that makes your order.
- **Port and logistics:** Solapur's towels usually ship through the Mumbai ports, Karur's through Chennai or Tuticorin, Panipat's usually through the western ports, in Gujarat or at Mumbai. The port affects the FOB point on your quotation.

## Questions to ask any Indian supplier

- Which factory makes this product, in which city, and is it yours or a partner's?
- What does that factory mainly make?
- Do the certificates you have sent name that factory?
- Can I see a physical sample made there before bulk production?
- Which port will the goods ship from?
