Buyers researching organic cotton usually start by asking for a price difference, and the honest answer is that the quote changes for reasons well beyond raw material cost — starting with which product line can supply it at all.

## The first question is which product, not which price

Our cotton towel range is woven at our manufacturing partner V P Mundada in Solapur, certified under OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100, MADE IN GREEN and STeP, ISO 9001:2015, GRS and amfori BSCI. Organic cotton bed and table linen is a separate line, made by our partner Cotonex in Karur and certified under GOTS Version 7.0. These are two different mills, two different certifications, and asking for "organic cotton" doesn't automatically move between them — a buyer wanting an organic-cotton towel is asking for something outside our current range, and we'd say so plainly rather than blur the line between a GOTS-certified linen product and a conventionally certified towel.

## What GOTS actually adds to a quotation

For the organic linen line, a GOTS Version 7.0 certificate means the fibre is tracked from certified organic farming through spinning, weaving, dyeing and finishing, with restricted chemical inputs at every stage — and that chain of custody is itself part of what a buyer is paying for, not just the raw cotton. A serious compliance team will ask for the certificate scope and transaction certificate for the specific order, not just a general claim, and that documentation is prepared as a standard part of quoting the line, not assembled after the fact.

## Why lead time can move too

Organic cotton fibre sourcing runs on its own supply calendar, separate from the conventional cotton the towel mill uses, and certified organic processing capacity is a narrower slice of any mill's total capacity. Depending on the season and the quantity requested, this can mean a longer lead time than a conventional order of the same size — worth asking about upfront rather than assuming it matches a standard towel timeline.

## What doesn't change

Sample-first still applies exactly the same way: a physical sample is developed and approved before bulk production, regardless of which certification the fibre carries. The quotation process itself — product, quantity, destination, then a proposed specification — doesn't skip a step for an organic request; it just draws on a different mill and a different certificate to answer it.

## What to actually ask for

If organic cotton matters to your programme, the useful question isn't "what's the organic surcharge" in the abstract — it's which product you need it on, since that determines whether we're quoting our GOTS-certified linen line or telling you honestly that it isn't currently part of the towel range.

Tell us the product and whether organic certification is a requirement, and our export team will confirm what can actually be supplied against it — and what to expect on lead time — before you commit to anything.
