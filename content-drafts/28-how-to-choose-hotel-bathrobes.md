A hotel bathrobe is specified by five decisions: the collar, the fabric, the weight, the size and the finishing. Most of the cost and most of the guest's impression come from the first two. Most of the complaints after delivery come from the last two: a robe that shrinks a size in the first wash, or one that only fits half the guests. This guide takes the five in order and ends with what to send a manufacturer so the quote describes the robe you actually want.

It is general guidance for writing your own specification. The weight ranges below are common industry ranges, not figures for any robe we make. Ours are confirmed on quotation, against your brief.

## 1. Collar: shawl, kimono or hooded

The collar is the first thing a guest sees on the hook and the first thing they feel on the neck.

- **Shawl collar:** a rolled collar that runs from the neck down the front opening. It sits thicker and reads as classic hotel. It uses more fabric and more sewing than a kimono, so it usually costs more.
- **Kimono collar:** a flat band that runs around the neck and down the front. It is lighter, lies flat, packs smaller and costs less to make. It has become the usual choice for spas and boutique hotels, and for waffle robes in particular.
- **Hooded:** a hood in place of a collar. Common for pool, beach and children's robes, less so for an in-room hotel robe.

Choose the collar for how the robe is positioned, not only for price. A shawl collar on a thin robe looks like a compromise; a kimono collar on a heavy terry robe can look unfinished.

## 2. Fabric: terry, velour or waffle

The fabric decides how the robe performs: how much water it takes, how heavy it hangs, how fast it dries in the laundry.

| Fabric | What it is | Good for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terry | Loops on both faces, like a bath towel | A real post-bath robe that dries the guest | Heavy, slower to dry, takes more laundry capacity |
| Velour | Terry with the outer loops sheared to a soft, flat pile | A smoother, more upmarket look, and embroidery that sits cleanly | The sheared face absorbs less than looped terry; the inner face does the drying |
| Waffle | A flat weave with a raised grid of small pockets | Spas, gyms, warm climates, robes worn between treatments | Absorbs less than terry; the grid can shrink more if not pre-shrunk |

A spa that launders robes many times a day usually gains more from a fast-drying waffle than from a heavy terry. A hotel that leaves one robe in each room for a stay usually wants terry or velour, because the guest uses it straight from the shower.

## 3. Weight

Robe weight is quoted two ways: as GSM, the weight of a square metre of the fabric, or as the weight of the whole finished robe. Ask which one a quote means. A GSM figure says nothing about how much fabric the pattern uses, so a heavy fabric cut as a short, narrow robe can weigh less than a lighter fabric cut long.

As a rough guide to what buyers ask for:

- Waffle robes are usually specified as light fabrics, often somewhere around 200 to 300 GSM.
- Terry and velour hotel robes are usually heavier, often somewhere around 350 to 500 GSM.

Heavier is not automatically better. A heavy robe feels generous on the hook, but it costs more per piece, more per wash and more to ship, and in a warm climate guests may not want it. Our hotel towel GSM guide sets out the same trade-off for towels; it applies to robes too.

## 4. Size, and size after washing

Hotels choose between one size for every room and a size range.

- **One size:** simplest for housekeeping and stock, and the usual choice for in-room robes. Specify it generously, because it has to fit most adult guests, and check it on a tall guest as well as an average one.
- **Size range (for example S to XL):** fits better and suits retail, spa uniforms and gift shops, but every size is another stock line to count, launder and reorder.

Whatever you choose, state the finished measurements you expect, not only a size letter. Size letters differ between factories and between countries. Give at least the length from the shoulder to the hem, the chest width and the sleeve length, with a tolerance.

Cotton shrinks in its first washes, and commercial laundries wash hot. Ask the manufacturer what shrinkage to expect and whether the measurements quoted are before or after washing. Then check the pre-shipment sample after washing it the way your laundry will. A robe that fits on delivery and is a size small after a month is the most common bathrobe complaint there is.

## 5. Finishing: the details that cost little and matter a lot

- **Belt and belt loops:** a belt that is sewn on at the back does not go missing in the laundry. Two sets of loops at different heights fit more guests.
- **Pockets:** two patch pockets are usual. Say if you want none, for example for a spa robe.
- **Hanger loop:** small, but it decides whether the robe hangs neatly on a hook or is folded on a shelf.
- **Cuffs and hems:** turned-back cuffs read more finished; check that hems are double-stitched, because they are the first thing to fray.
- **Embroidery:** give the logo file, its size, its position (left chest is usual) and the thread colour. On terry, a dense logo can sink into the loops, which is one reason velour is popular for branded robes.
- **Colour:** white is the hotel default because it can be bleached. A colour needs a lab dip approved before dyeing, and it must survive your laundry's chemistry. Our guide to lab dips covers how that approval works.

## Matching robes to the towel programme

Robes are rarely bought alone. If your bath towels, hand towels and bath mats come from one mill and your robes from another, the white will not quite match and the reorders will drift apart. Specifying them together, with one colour standard and one sample approval, avoids that.

## What to send for a quote

A quote is only as precise as the brief. Send:

- Where the robe is used: in-room, spa, pool, retail or gift shop
- Collar and fabric: for example, shawl collar in velour, or kimono collar in waffle
- Target weight, and whether you mean fabric GSM or finished robe weight
- Sizes and the finished measurements you expect, with a tolerance
- Colour, and your laundry conditions if you know them
- Logo file, size and position, and any labels
- Packing: folded, on hangers, in individual bags, cartons per size
- Quantities per size, and the destination port
